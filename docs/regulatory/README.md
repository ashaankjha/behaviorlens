## Status statement

These documents are **draft engineering inputs** written by the development team for BehaviorLens, a local-first research/demo prototype that suggests observable movement events in staged videos for human review. They are structured after IEC 62304, ISO 14971, IEC 62366 and premarket cybersecurity practice, to make a future regulatory effort faster. They are **not** a regulatory submission, a design history file or a technical file.

BehaviorLens has **no** regulatory clearance, approval, CE marking or certification. It does not conform to any of the standards named here, and it has **no clinical validation**. It must not be used for clinical decision-making (`config.DISCLAIMER`). No manufacturer, quality management system or accountable regulatory owner has been designated. Every "Owner" field reads "TBD (manufacturer)" for that reason.

## What exists today (engineering evidence)

- Automated tests for event rules, storage integrity (migrations, hash-chained audit trail, tamper detection), exports, input robustness, logging redaction and the UI (Streamlit AppTest).
- CI on every push: ruff, mypy (strict), pytest with coverage and an enforced floor (`fail_under = 91`; 91.57% line+branch measured on 2026-10-20), `pip-audit` on pinned dependencies (lint, type-check, test, coverage and audit tools are also pinned in `constraints.txt`), and a Docker build with a loopback/non-root/model-hash smoke test. The Docker image is built only in CI, not as part of local verification.
- Real-video engineering measurements on small public research samples. Held-out `standing_transition` for the shipped rules-v0.3: recall 0.20 (2/10), precision 0.40 (2/5), 3 false positives (rules-v0.2: 0.40, 0.40, 6). These are engineering evidence, **not clinical validation** ([VALIDATION.md](../VALIDATION.md)).
- An independent clean-room audit with every discrepancy resolved ([AUDIT.md](../AUDIT.md)).

## What remains before any clinical use

1. **Regulatory strategy and classification.** Decide whether BehaviorLens is a medical device at all, under FDA SaMD / device software functions and clinical decision support criteria in the US and under EU MDR (Rule 11) in the EU, based on a finalised intended use. Then determine the classification and pathway, or document why it is not a device.
2. **Certified quality management system.** An ISO 13485 QMS run by a designated manufacturer, with design controls (design inputs and outputs, reviews, verification, validation, transfer, design history file) and document control.
3. **IEC 62304 process executed, not just drafted.** Software safety classification, an approved development plan, requirements and architecture under change control, unit and integration verification records, SOUP management, problem resolution, and release and maintenance processes.
4. **ISO 14971 risk management owned by the manufacturer.** An approved risk management plan with acceptability criteria, risk controls verified, an overall residual-risk and benefit-risk determination, and a risk management report. See the gaps listed in [risk_management.md](risk_management.md) §5.
5. **IEC 62366 usability engineering.** Use specification, user-interface evaluation, formative studies, and a summative human-factors study with representative BCBAs, focused on automation bias and on understanding that suggestions are not exhaustive.
6. **Clinical validation.** Execute a finalised version of [clinical_validation_plan.md](clinical_validation_plan.md) under IRB/ethics approval on real clinical recordings, with pre-specified acceptance criteria and a locked algorithm.
7. **Cybersecurity per FDA premarket guidance** (and equivalent EU expectations): authentication and access control, a per-user audit trail, a machine-readable SBOM, a vulnerability management and coordinated disclosure process, a threat model reviewed by security professionals, and penetration testing. See [cybersecurity.md](cybersecurity.md) §13.
8. **Data protection.** A HIPAA (US) and GDPR (EU) assessment, including a DPIA, lawful basis and consent, retention and deletion covering backups, encryption at rest enforced by the deployment, and business associate or processor agreements where applicable.
9. **Post-market surveillance.** Complaint handling, vigilance and adverse-event reporting, field performance monitoring (including drift after any model or dependency update), and periodic safety or post-market reports as the pathway requires.
10. **Labelling.** Instructions for use (camera protocol: fixed camera, full body visible, single person), the limitations and non-claims, warnings that the output is not an exhaustive record, and UDI and regulatory markings once a pathway is chosen.
